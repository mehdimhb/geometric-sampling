# Simulation implementation brief

The main requested implementation is in place. The full replicated study is still running in the small-N stage. Completed validation demonstrates numerical constraint preservation, but does not establish general exact recovery by the primary optimizer.

1. **Your package is the primary algorithm.** The study calls the existing `graphical_sampling.search.GreedyBestFirstSearch.run`. Its neighbor generation, frontier, pruning and nine-decimal score comparisons are retained. The supplied criterion is target-pair RMSE. Study settings include frontier size 50, exchange coefficient 0.75, a single global zone, and backtracking depth zero. These are explicit study settings, not a claim to use every package default. Original package files and existing tracked user edits remain unchanged. An external float64 adapter preserves small probability fragments down to 1e-15, provides reproducible random streams, and conditions certainty units out of the graphical builder before appending them to every sample.

2. **The objective matches the request.** For active sample probabilities, induced moments are
   \[
   \pi_i(p)=\sum_s p_s I(i\in s),\qquad
   \pi_{ij}(p)=\sum_s p_s I(i,j\in s).
   \]
   The loss is \(D_2=\sum_{i<j}w_{ij}(\pi_{ij}(p)-\pi^*_{ij})^2\), with weights one for all pairs or zero for omitted sparse pairs. Full-pair RMSE is \(\sqrt{D_2/\binom N2}\) for full targeting. Sparse target-pair RMSE uses \(|E|\) as denominator; full-pair error is also independently computed against the underlying complete target. Maximum pair error is recorded.

3. **Your exchanges and epsilon formula are implemented.** An exchange subtracts epsilon from samples a,b and adds it to the two swapped samples, with \(0\leq\epsilon\leq\min(p_a,p_b)\). Probability changes sum to zero; the combined unit-indicator vector is unchanged, preserving FIP and sample size. For r different from k,l,
   \[
   \Delta_{kr}=\epsilon[I(r\in b)-I(r\in a)],\quad
   \Delta_{lr}=-\Delta_{kr},\quad\Delta_{kl}=0.
   \]
   Additional Random, Greedy, Directed and Advanced searches are comparisons. Directed uses signed residuals; Advanced adds multiple starts and random shocks. They evaluate the exact quadratic change \(2A\epsilon+B\epsilon^2\), with \(A=\sum w_{ij}(\pi_{ij}-\pi^*_{ij})c_{ij}\), \(B=\sum w_{ij}c_{ij}^2\), and optimized \(\epsilon=\operatorname{clip}(-A/B,0,\min(p_a,p_b))\) when B>0. Random/fixed/optimized epsilon are ablated. **The primary package retains its existing exchange mechanism and 0.75 coefficient; optimized epsilon was not substituted into your optimizer.**

4. **Correctness is checked independently.** Tests verify exchange identities and optimized epsilon against a numerical grid. Every accepted additional-search move and every scored primary candidate is checked for valid size, nonnegative mass, probability sum and prescribed FIP, with tolerance 1e-10. Additional searches independently recompute SIP every 25 accepted moves and at finalization. The primary criterion recomputes SIP directly from support for every candidate. The executed suite passed 65 tests. In the completed 27-case longer validation, maximum errors were 1.40e-12 for FIP, 5.66e-14 for total probability and 4.45e-16 for incremental SIP. These checks certify valid designs, not target recovery.

5. **Small-N gold standards and competitors have explicit limits.** For N<=14, enumerate all n-subsets. LP imposes p>=0, sum(p)=1, prescribed FIP and SIP. For LP-infeasible targets, convex QP minimizes D2 under probability/FIP constraints. An independent linear oracle bounds QP suboptimality; certification requires primal error <=1e-10 and an absolute D2 gap <=1e-7. CP2-enumerated calibrates the specified exponential family with an exact partition function, using our L-BFGS/Newton calibration; it is not a reproduction of Bondesson's published iterative/Gibbs algorithm and is restricted to N<=14. Sinha uses a documented restricted nonnegative mixture; unsupported cases are marked not applicable. DSD uses the existing projection construction as a structured benchmark, not a general arbitrary-SIP solver.

6. **Experiments and reporting follow the requested design, subject to unfinished execution.** Targets are independent random, sparse-support, diffuse, SRS, conditional-Poisson, stratified and DSD designs, plus perturbed targets whose small-N feasibility is established by LP. Algorithms receive inclusion targets rather than hidden generating probabilities. All applicable methods share each target and deterministic saved seeds. Dedicated equally spaced line populations and planar settings are included; the current primary loss contains no spatial-distance penalty. The grid specifies 50 small-N, 30 moderate-N, and 10 large/sparse replications per scenario, up to N=1000. Budgets are 1,000 candidates/10-second safety cap for small N and 10,000/60 seconds later; separate wall-time runs use a larger candidate ceiling. These are finite budgets, not the full optional 100,000-candidate/300-second range. Scored designs for the package and scored exchanges for other searches are different work units. Raw failures, actual budgets, convergence, support, recovery tolerances, paired differences and uncertainty summaries are retained. Nine PDF/300-dpi PNG figure types and CSV/LaTeX tables are implemented; current published report figures describe pilots.

**Unfavorable finding that must remain visible:** the completed longer validation found primary package recovery at RMSE<1e-8 in 0/27 cases, versus 7/27 for Greedy, 7/27 for Directed and 26/27 for CP2-enumerated. This is finite-budget evidence, not a proof of impossibility or universal superiority. Final moderate/large scalability claims and the full replicated paper results remain unfinished.
